1. THE SHORT VERSION: WHAT I WANT FROM THE CALL
======================================================================

For a future commercial review, I want to determine whether Stax Connect could become a materially better replacement or secondary embedded-payments partner after Nitewide launches on Stripe Connect.

Stax is especially interesting because:

1. Nitewide is an Orlando startup launching first in Orlando and Florida.
2. Stax was founded in Orlando and maintains its headquarters in Orlando.
3. Stax Connect publicly supports embedded/white-label payments, submerchant enrollment and underwriting, APIs, partner webhooks, disputes, custom fees, and split funding.
4. A local partnership could give both companies a strong Orlando-to-Florida case study.

The decision cannot be based on location alone. To select Stax instead of Stripe Connect, Stax must provide:

- Better complete economics at our expected volume, not merely a lower headline rate.
- Written confirmation that each venue, organizer, or independent creator can be the seller and settlement merchant for its own transactions.
- A structure in which merchant refunds, disputes, chargeback fees, fines, taxes, and unrecovered merchant losses are not silently transferred to Nitewide.
- Onboarding and underwriting that work for both individuals/sole proprietors and legal business entities.
- Reliable APIs/webhooks for payments, refunds, disputes, deposits, merchant status, reconciliation, and evidence submission.
- A workable method for Nitewide's customer service fee and organizer-funded promoter rewards across cards and wallets.
- Competitive funds availability and payout timing with accurate language about holds and bank arrival.
- Named implementation, underwriting/risk, and incident contacts for an Orlando pilot.
- Data export, token portability, and a practical exit/migration path.

If Stax cannot meet these requirements in writing by our provider-decision deadline, Nitewide will use Stripe Connect as the schedule-safe fallback.

3. NITEWIDE PRODUCT AND BUSINESS MODEL
======================================================================

Nitewide serves four overlapping groups:

- Customers discovering events, buying tickets/packages, requesting guestlist access, and presenting QR credentials.
- Venues and organizations managing events, inventory, employees, guests, sales, check-in, reporting, and payouts.
- Independent event creators who may sell without forming or joining an organization.
- Promoters/affiliates who receive organizer-funded referral rewards and may also receive guestlist allocations.

One Nitewide user can hold several roles simultaneously. The payments model therefore cannot assume that every person is only a customer or only a business operator.

Current product pricing:

- Customer service fee: 7.5% of face-value subtotal plus $0.79 once per paid order.
- Free organization plan: no Nitewide subscription or organizer-side platform transaction fee.
- Optional Premium: $249 per month for advanced analytics, promoter ROI, CRM/marketing, and management capabilities; no transaction-fee discount.
- Promoter monetization: the organizer funds the gross promoter reward; Nitewide retains 10% of that reward and the promoter receives 90% before adjustments.
- Current approved implementation policy: the organizer pays actual payment-processing cost.
- Deferred sensitivity only: Nitewide absorbs routine processing so organizers pay no routine processing or platform fees. This is not the active roadmap and would require a separately approved provider contract, unit-economics model, legal review, and risk plan.

Every event sale is paid in full at checkout. Nitewide does not currently plan to advance unsettled funds.

10. FRAUD, QR CHECK-IN, AND DISPUTE MANAGEMENT
======================================================================

Nitewide will create an auditable evidence chain:

provider transaction -> Nitewide order -> order item -> ticket/admission credential -> signed opaque QR -> check-in record

The QR contains an opaque, revocable credential, not payment IDs or customer data. Check-in records include event, entrance, timestamp, scanner/device, authorized operator, credential/key version, online/offline state, and reasoned manual overrides.

CONFIRMED PUBLICLY:

- Stax documents dispute retrieval, filters, response deadlines, evidence uploads, status tracking, and dispute webhooks.
- Documented dispute states include INQUIRY, OPEN, PENDING, EVIDENCE_UPLOADED, PREARBITRATION, WON, LOST, and ACCEPTED.
- Public documentation supports JPEG, PDF, PNG, and TIFF evidence files up to 10MB per document.
- Stax partner webhooks include dispute creation; merchant-status and underwriting webhooks are also documented.
- Stax states that the merchant is notified and may provide evidence to challenge a dispute.

NITEWIDE IMPLEMENTATION COMMITMENTS:

- Verify and idempotently persist every relevant webhook before acknowledging it.
- Build a reason-specific evidence packet containing transaction/authentication results, receipt, accepted terms, refund policy, credential lifecycle, QR scan/check-in, lawful customer/account signals, communications, and refund/transfer history.
- Notify authorized venue operators through a secure task; never email raw IDs, full evidence packets, QR secrets, or sensitive registration records.
- Use the actual processor response deadline, not a fixed seven-day assumption.
- Require human review before submitting evidence.
- Apply reversible risk restrictions to disputed customer accounts; do not automatically label a customer fraudulent merely because a dispute was created.
- Track dispute outcome, merchant debit, recovery, restrictions, and ledger adjustments.

QUESTIONS FOR STAX:

1. Which dispute lifecycle events are available by partner webhook beyond creation?
2. Can Nitewide retrieve and submit evidence for all submerchants with properly scoped partner credentials?
3. Can the venue also self-serve disputes in a Stax-hosted or embedded experience?
4. Does the API expose exact response deadlines, reason codes, debited amount, chargeback fee, funding account, and final recovery?
5. Can QR/check-in records and customer communications be submitted as structured or PDF evidence?
6. When exactly is the merchant debited, and how is a winning dispute reinstated?
7. What fraud tools and chargeback-monitoring programs are included?
8. Who handles pre-arbitration and card-network monitoring thresholds?
9. What are the chargeback, retrieval, evidence, and arbitration fees?
10. What are the escalation contacts and response SLAs during a live event or material fraud incident?

- The effective cost is not materially better than Stripe after all fees and reserves.
- Nitewide must guarantee all submerchant losses or maintain an uneconomic reserve.
- The venue cannot be the seller/settlement merchant for its own event sales.
- Negative-balance and failed-recovery responsibility is ambiguous.
- Platform/customer fees cannot be collected reliably on Apple Pay or Google Pay.
- Promoter reward accounting cannot be reversed and reconciled.
- Independent creators/sole proprietors or nightlife/event sellers are ineligible.
- Onboarding forces Nitewide to retain raw SSNs, IDs, bank details, or full payment credentials without a compelling reason.
- Required merchant, underwriting, dispute, deposit, or settlement events are not available by API/webhook.
- There is no usable partner sandbox for failure and remediation states.
- Funding-speed claims are marketing language without contractual definitions and reporting.
- Data, tokens, and merchant records cannot be exported or migrated on termination.
- Long exclusivity, auto-renewal, minimums, or termination fees remove Nitewide's safe fallback.
- Stax cannot provide named implementation and risk escalation support for the pilot.

17. INTERNAL DECISION RULE AFTER THE CALL
======================================================================

Do not select Stax on the call.

After receiving written materials:

1. Score Stax against Stripe using the repository provider scorecard.
2. Model total cost at the actual proposed card/wallet mix and all rollout stages.
3. Have counsel review seller/MoR, merchant loss, reserves, recovery, fee disclosure, money movement, tax, privacy, indemnity, and termination.
4. Prove onboarding, payments, fees, refunds, disputes, payouts, negative balances, and reconciliation in sandbox.
5. Verify that the platform-fee mechanism works with Apple Pay and Google Pay.
6. Run failure cases, not only successful payments.
7. Approve a provider/account-model architecture decision record and rollback plan.
8. Begin limited live processing only after all launch blockers are closed.

Stax should be selected only if it produces better risk-adjusted economics than Stripe while matching Nitewide's required compliance, merchant isolation, payments, fraud/dispute, wallet, reporting, and operational capabilities.
