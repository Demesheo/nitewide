# Guestlist invitations and in-app notifications

## What works now

From the Business app's Events → event details → Share this event card, an organization owner or manager, or an independent event creator, can invite a guest into the event's direct pool. They can also use their own event allocation when one is active. Employees and promoters can invite only into their own active event allocation, and only when its effective limit is greater than zero. The invitation form takes a guest name and 1–20 spots. Personal invitations require only a name; Email and Phone add the selected contact. The host copies the private link and shares it themselves. The API checks permissions and available people-capacity, not just the browser. Request review and capacity controls are in that event's Guestlist subtab.

Every newly created invitation is approved immediately and reserves its spots in the selected pool, in the same event-locked transaction. Full pools reject creation rather than sending an unreserved promise. An existing active account matching the email remains attached; a phone number auto-matches only one active account with that **verified** number. Unknown recipients and personal invitations have no account ID; no synthetic accounts are created. Recipients open the private link without registering or signing in. Four spots produce **four separate, single-use QR passes**; guests may arrive separately. Reopening or simultaneously claiming the link does not create additional approvals or passes. Personal bearer links never silently attach to an arbitrary signed-in account.

Anyone possessing the link can view all its passes. Treat the URL as an admission secret: share privately, do not log it, cache it, or expose the full URL in Business UI. The Guest experience details modal provides **Copy link** for authorized reviewers; the high-level cards and table rows do not. A domain-separated HMAC admission token regenerates that same link without storing its plaintext secret. The guest page sends no referrer and refreshes admission status while visible; QR availability is checked against approval, event and venue lifecycle, publication, and the existing admission expiry window. Already issued admissions retain door continuity under an organization suspension, consistent with the admissions policy. Account creation is optional and does not gate entry. Each pass is scanned independently under event/entry/pass locks; duplicate scans return a conflict, partial admission counts remain exact, and a parent group token cannot bypass separate passes.

Legacy pending invitations retain their original expiry and capacity-at-claim behavior. Claim still checks the event, organization, venue, inviter and current access before issuing any new approval. Already-issued legacy group QR codes are preserved rather than split or rotated.

Removing a person from an organization or deactivating their organization-scoped event allocation revokes their outstanding **pending legacy invitations** in the same transaction and blocks new issuance. Re-adding that person restores their ability to issue new invitations, but never reactivates revoked pending links. **Already approved invitations keep their admissions**—removing a staff member is not an implicit cancellation of confirmed guests. An authorized reviewer can explicitly revoke an unused approval, invalidating every pass and releasing its spots. Once any pass has been admitted, the existing no-revocation rule prevents releasing already-used capacity. Reapproval regenerates the parent QR binding, so screenshots of old revoked passes remain invalid. A separately granted, event-scoped promoter allocation remains independent of organization membership.

Both apps have a signed-in notification inbox with unread counts and mark-as-read. The server currently records guestlist requests for their authorized reviewer, approval/decline/revocation for customers, confirmed invitations for customers, purchase confirmations, and sold-out offering alerts for organization owners/managers or independent creators. Notifications are created transactionally with the underlying action and are scoped to the recipient. They are not emails or texts. The API lists the newest 50 non-dismissed records per user; retention/pagination need a production policy.

Customer booking notifications open **Booked** directly to the referenced purchase or guestlist entry and its QR credentials, not the event sales dialog. Purchase notifications use `metadata.orderId`; approvals and accepted invitations use `metadata.entryId`. Old invitation records missing an entry ID are resolved at read time against that recipient's own event guestlist entry. Deep links retrieve the exact booking independently of list pagination and select the matching upcoming/past period. Missing or unauthorized records show an error and retain the notification.

Once a booking opens successfully, its notification is dismissed from the inbox. **Clear all**, at the upper left opposite the close button, dismisses all of the signed-in user's notifications, including records beyond the current 50-item list. Migration `202609230002-notification-dismissal` adds nullable `dismissed_at`; records are retained, not deleted. Dismissal survives refresh and excludes records from unread counts. Both dismissal endpoints require authentication and scope to the recipient. Business notification navigation is unchanged.

Migration `202610020001-account-free-guestlist` makes recipient accounts optional, adds guest contact snapshots and individual pass rows, backfills historical admitted counts and accepted invitation links, and extends check-in credential integrity. It does not reseed or rotate existing QR codes. Its downgrade intentionally refuses to discard admission history. The isolated integration suite covers anonymous access, four distinct QR passes, repeated claims, authorization, forged links, capacity races, partial/duplicate scans, revocation and archived-event QR suppression. Existing suites retain legacy pending-invitation lifecycle and group-pass coverage. Playwright exercises invitation creation, copying again, and the public pass page on phone and desktop; emails and external services are mocked/blocked.

## Provider integration path and launch gates

1. Add an append-only notification outbox with an idempotency key, delivery attempts, retries, dead-letter state, and per-channel templates. An in-app record is the source of truth; a worker sends email/SMS asynchronously. Do not send inside the purchase or guestlist transaction.
2. Verify email ownership and phone possession before relying on contact matches or sending sensitive links. Today email signup has no verification, so immediate email-account matching is an MVP trust limitation. A private invite link is a bearer secret and must be treated like one. Phone-only invitations are never auto-matched to an unverified existing number. Normalize/uniquely claim verified numbers and handle changed or shared numbers.
3. Add email delivery (transactional provider), then Twilio SMS behind the existing `transactionalSmsConsentAt` and `phoneVerifiedAt` fields. Invitation contact information supplied by a host is **not** the recipient's SMS marketing consent. Marketing messages require separate `marketingSmsConsentAt`/`marketingConsentAt`, unsubscribe and suppression lists; no marketing send is implemented here.
4. Add scheduled reminders based on event-local time, check-in/purchase state, opt-outs, quiet hours, reschedules and cancellations. Include actionable pending-approval reminders and sold-out alerts without flooding a sole operator. Make jobs idempotent and record delivery results.
5. Customer Booked now retrieves purchase and guestlist QR credentials from the authenticated API. Harden recovery and production door-entry operations before live rollout. Never place QR secrets in notification text or logs. Also add invite cancellation/reissue and abuse throttles before broad rollout.

Business invitation endpoints are `GET /api/business/events/:eventId/guestlist-invite-pools`, `POST /api/business/events/:eventId/guestlist-invitations`, and `GET /api/business/events/:eventId/guestlist/:entryId/invitation-link`. Bearer-link endpoints are public `POST /api/guestlist-invitations/:token/claim` and `GET /api/guestlist-invitations/:token/pass`; they expose only that invitation's admission view, not recipient contact/account data. Both are no-store and IP throttled. Registration retains optional `guestlistInviteToken` for contact-matched account linking. The authenticated inbox uses `GET /api/notifications`, `POST /api/notifications/:id/read`, `DELETE /api/notifications/:id` (dismiss one), and `DELETE /api/notifications` (clear all). DELETE soft-dismisses records only; it never changes the booking, tickets, or guestlist allocation.
